# Learnability Section: Understanding ROAS, ACOS and TACOS

## Objective

The purpose of this section is to understand three key Amazon Advertising metrics:

1. ROAS (Return on Ad Spend)
2. ACOS (Advertising Cost of Sale)
3. TACOS (Total Advertising Cost of Sale)

Rather than memorizing definitions, I focused on understanding how these metrics influence advertising decisions, profitability, and long-term business growth.

---

# 1. ROAS (Return on Ad Spend)

## Definition

ROAS measures how much revenue is generated for every rupee spent on advertising.

Formula:

ROAS = Ad Revenue ÷ Ad Spend

---

## Example

Suppose a brand spends ₹1,00,000 on Amazon ads.

The ads generate ₹4,00,000 in revenue.

ROAS = ₹4,00,000 ÷ ₹1,00,000

ROAS = 4

This means every ₹1 spent on advertising generated ₹4 in revenue.

---

## Why Brands Care About ROAS

For a brand spending ₹15 lakh per month on advertising, ROAS is one of the fastest indicators of campaign efficiency.

It helps answer questions such as:

- Are advertising campaigns generating enough revenue?
- Which products deserve more budget?
- Which keywords are wasting spend?
- Is campaign performance improving or declining?

A higher ROAS generally indicates that advertising spend is being used efficiently.

However, ROAS alone does not tell whether the business is profitable because it ignores product costs, Amazon fees, and operating expenses.

---

## What Is a Good ROAS?

There is no universal answer.

A "good" ROAS depends on category economics and profit margins.

Examples:

### Premium Skincare

- Higher product margins
- Can tolerate lower ROAS
- Customer lifetime value may be high

Example:

ROAS = 3 may still be profitable.

---

### Consumer Electronics

- Lower margins
- Heavy competition
- Higher advertising costs

Example:

ROAS = 3 may not be profitable.

A brand may need ROAS above 5 to achieve healthy profitability.

---

# 2. ACOS (Advertising Cost of Sale)

## Definition

ACOS measures advertising spend as a percentage of advertising-generated revenue.

Formula:

ACOS = Ad Spend ÷ Ad Revenue × 100

---

## Example

Ad Spend = ₹1,00,000

Ad Revenue = ₹4,00,000

ACOS = ₹1,00,000 ÷ ₹4,00,000 × 100

ACOS = 25%

This means the brand spent ₹25 on advertising for every ₹100 generated through ads.

---

## Why Brands Care About ACOS

ACOS helps evaluate advertising efficiency from a cost perspective.

It answers questions such as:

- How much of revenue is being consumed by advertising?
- Can the product margins support current ad spending?
- Which campaigns are profitable?
- Which campaigns need optimization?

Many Amazon advertising teams use ACOS as a primary operating metric because it directly links advertising spend to sales performance.

---

## Relationship Between ACOS and ROAS

ROAS and ACOS measure the same performance from different perspectives.

They are mathematical inverses.

ROAS = 100 ÷ ACOS

ACOS = 100 ÷ ROAS

Examples:

| ROAS | ACOS |
|------|------|
| 2 | 50% |
| 4 | 25% |
| 5 | 20% |
| 10 | 10% |

As ROAS increases, ACOS decreases.

As ACOS increases, ROAS decreases.

Both metrics communicate campaign efficiency but in different formats.

---

## When Is High ACOS Acceptable?

A high ACOS is not always bad.

Examples:

### Product Launch

When introducing a new product, a brand may intentionally accept a higher ACOS to:

- Generate initial sales
- Build rankings
- Collect reviews
- Increase visibility

Short-term profitability may be sacrificed for long-term growth.

---

### Brand Awareness

If the goal is acquiring new customers rather than immediate profit, a higher ACOS may be acceptable.

---

## When Is High ACOS a Problem?

High ACOS becomes problematic when:

- Product margins cannot support advertising costs
- Profitability declines continuously
- Sales growth is not improving
- Organic visibility is not increasing

In these situations advertising spend is not creating sustainable business value.

---

# 3. TACOS (Total Advertising Cost of Sale)

## Definition

TACOS measures advertising spend as a percentage of total business revenue, including both paid and organic sales.

Formula:

TACOS = Ad Spend ÷ Total Revenue × 100

Where:

Total Revenue = Paid Revenue + Organic Revenue

---

## Example

Ad Spend = ₹1,00,000

Ad Revenue = ₹4,00,000

Organic Revenue = ₹6,00,000

Total Revenue = ₹10,00,000

TACOS = ₹1,00,000 ÷ ₹10,00,000 × 100

TACOS = 10%

---

## Why TACOS Is Different From ACOS

ACOS only evaluates advertising-generated revenue.

TACOS evaluates the health of the overall Amazon business.

This distinction is important because advertising should not only create immediate sales.

Advertising should also:

- Improve product ranking
- Increase brand awareness
- Generate reviews
- Improve organic discoverability

These effects often increase organic sales over time.

ACOS may remain unchanged while TACOS improves significantly because organic revenue grows.

---

## Why TACOS Is a Better Long-Term Metric

TACOS captures the relationship between advertising investment and total business growth.

Example:

### Month 1

Ad Spend = ₹1,00,000

Ad Revenue = ₹4,00,000

Organic Revenue = ₹2,00,000

Total Revenue = ₹6,00,000

TACOS = 16.7%

---

### Month 6

Ad Spend = ₹1,00,000

Ad Revenue = ₹4,00,000

Organic Revenue = ₹8,00,000

Total Revenue = ₹12,00,000

TACOS = 8.3%

Advertising spend remained constant.

Paid sales remained constant.

Organic sales increased significantly.

As a result TACOS improved dramatically.

This indicates that advertising is creating long-term business value beyond immediate sales.

---

# Key Takeaways

## ROAS

Measures revenue generated from advertising spend.

Useful for understanding campaign efficiency.

---

## ACOS

Measures advertising cost relative to advertising revenue.

Useful for evaluating advertising profitability.

---

## TACOS

Measures advertising cost relative to total business revenue.

Useful for evaluating long-term business health.

---

# My Understanding

The most important insight I gained is that these metrics should not be viewed independently.

ROAS helps measure revenue efficiency.

ACOS helps measure advertising efficiency.

TACOS helps measure business health.

A company focused only on ACOS may underinvest in growth opportunities.

A company focused only on ROAS may overlook profitability.

A company monitoring TACOS alongside ROAS and ACOS can better understand whether advertising is creating sustainable growth, stronger organic performance, and long-term profitability.
